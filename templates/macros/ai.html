{# AI transparency labelling. The vocabulary here follows the seams of the EU AI Act's Article 50 rather than a homegrown "was AI used, yes/no" scale, because the Act draws its lines in places a boolean cannot reach: none No AI involved in the text. assisted AI used as an assistive function for standard editing, without substantially altering the input data or its semantics — the language of the Art. 50(2) exemption. The author wrote it. edited AI produced material that a human then reviewed, with a named person holding editorial responsibility. This is the Art. 50(4) exception, and stating it is the point: the exception is only available to someone who will put their name to the review. generated AI-generated text published without human editorial review — the case where Art. 50(4) makes disclosure mandatory. Images run on their own track, because Art. 50(4)'s first subparagraph is about the picture rather than about where it sits on the page — a generated diagram halfway down a post is as in scope as the one at the top. So there are three levels, narrowing as they go: [extra] ai_image a claim about the article's imagery as a whole. Puts a sentence in the note at the foot, and supplies the default posture for the featured image. [extra] image_ai the featured image alone, overriding the above. "none" opts the hero out of a post-level claim. {{ figure(ai=…) }} one body image, stated at the point of use. Never inherits: a blanket claim shouldn't silently credit a photograph somebody took with a camera. Article 50(5) requires the disclosure to be given "in a clear and distinguishable manner at the latest at the time of the first interaction or exposure", which is why the label sits in the article's metadata line and in the feed item, not only in the note at the foot of the page. A reader in an RSS client may never load the page at all. Every string has an English fallback here, so the theme works with no configuration; a site overrides any of them under [extra.ai_transparency]. The keys are deliberately flat (`label_edited` rather than a nested `[extra.ai_transparency.edited] label`) — a distributed theme meets half-filled config all the time, and a flat key with a `default` filter degrades where a dynamic lookup into a missing table raises. #} {# --- Metadata-line label ----------------------------------------------- Renders its own leading separator, so callers can drop it into a metadata line without knowing whether it will produce anything. #} {% macro meta_label(page, config) %} {%- if config.extra.ai_transparency is defined -%} {%- set at = config.extra.ai_transparency -%} {%- set posture = page.extra.ai_text | default(value=at.default_text | default(value="none")) -%} {%- if posture != "none" or at.show_when_none | default(value=false) -%} · {%- if posture == "generated" %}{{ at.label_generated | default(value="AI-generated") | safe }} {%- elif posture == "edited" %}{{ at.label_edited | default(value="AI-drafted, human-edited") | safe }} {%- elif posture == "assisted" %}{{ at.label_assisted | default(value="AI-assisted") | safe }} {%- else %}{{ at.label_none | default(value="Written without AI") | safe }} {%- endif %} {%- endif -%} {%- endif -%} {% endmacro meta_label %} {# --- Note at the foot of the article ------------------------------------ The expansion of the label above. `aria-labelledby` points at the kicker so the region is announced as what it is; Art. 50(5) also requires the disclosure to meet accessibility requirements, which is why the label is real text rather than an icon or a title attribute. #} {# The disclosure prose itself, with no markup around it, so the article note and the feed item can never drift apart.
